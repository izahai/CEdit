# Few-style erasure evaluation with 100-rank style subspace

The aggregate values are from the server's `summary.csv`; the matching
per-content results and run fingerprints are preserved in `detailed_metrics.csv`.

> The workflow completed on `root@61.32.91.194`, SSH port `18802`.
> SPEED uses threshold `0.1`, `aug_num=10`, and anchor `art`. Ours uses TGPRS
> with target embeddings computed from prompts suffixed by `style`, threshold
> `0.3`, `retain_scale=1.0`, `aug_num=0`, anchor `art`, and 100 artist-neutral
> style anchors (configured and applied rank 100). Sampling uses seed 0,
> DPM-Solver, 20 denoising steps, and CFG 7.5. Each artist metric uses 30 images
> and each MS-COCO metric uses 100 images. The run contains 1,750 PNG files and
> six edited checkpoints.

Lower target CLIP score indicates stronger erasure. Lower non-target and MS-COCO
FID and higher MS-COCO CLIP score indicate better preservation. Bold values are
the better edited-model result within each task.

## Result summary

With 100 style anchors and rank 100, TGPRS reduces target CLIP score relative
to the unedited model for Picasso and Monet by 1.14 and 1.86 points,
respectively. For Van Gogh, its target score increases by 0.77 points. SPEED
produces stronger erasure in all three tasks, with target scores lower than
TGPRS by 4.20 for Van Gogh, 1.18 for Picasso, and 2.71 for Monet.

TGPRS has substantially worse mean non-target FID than SPEED: higher by 90.55
for Van Gogh, 75.90 for Picasso, and 86.46 for Monet. Its MS-COCO CLIP score is
0.211 points lower than SPEED for Van Gogh, but 0.347 and 0.405 points higher for
Picasso and Monet. TGPRS has worse MS-COCO FID in all three tasks, by 2.66,
7.09, and 7.79 points, respectively.
